# 1959 — the two-minute film

In 1959 David Hubel and Torsten Wiesel lowered an electrode into the visual
cortex of a cat and moved a bar of light across its field of view. A single
neuron answered, and its spikes were audible as clicks on tape. This film is
the story of that same recording being played, sixty-seven years later, into a
fabricated SkyWater 130 nm die holding sixteen analog spiking neurons: every
click found on the tape becomes one spike delivered to neuron 14 through its
excitatory synapse, and the membrane that answers is what the installation
draws as its spiral. The last beat is the piece itself — the cat's cortex in
one ear, the silicon in the other, almost in unison.
